# Art direction (starting point)

Consistent and funny matters more than pretty. Most of our art will be AI-generated or pulled from free packs, and assets from different sources look like they came from different games unless we hold them to the same rules. This file is those rules. Expect it to change after the first playtest.

## Style

- Low poly with flat colors or very simple textures, drawn from one limited palette. The palette gets decided in M6 and saved as `assets/palette.png`.
- Readable silhouettes beat detail. If something is funny, make sure it reads from 20 meters away.
- Characters are simple and a bit goofy. Physical comedy (wobbly ragdolls, exaggerated stumbles) does more work than facial detail.

## How models are made right now

- Models are built in code, not imported: `props/mesh_kit.gd` makes lumpy blobs, rounded boxes, bent tubes, cones, wheels, and leaf cards, merged into one mesh with shading baked into vertex colors, drawn by `props/stylized.gd`. Buildings and ground keep the procedural surface shader (`props/surfaces.gd`).
- Look at a model before shipping it: `Godot --path . res://tools/studio/studio.tscn -- --subject=trees --out=build/art/trees.png` renders four angles under the game's light. Add a subject in `tools/studio/studio.gd` for anything new.
- Shapes are soft and chunky: rounded edges, lumps that only push outward on hair and hedges, no part floating loose or poking through another. Faces have eyes, brows, and mouths flush with the head.

### Houses and the kit

- **Architecture goes through HouseKit** (`props/house_kit.gd`): `box`, `prism` (a gable along local z, like PrismMesh), `hip`, `cylinder` (with `top_radius` and `turn`), `window`, `glow`, then `commit(parent, name)` makes one MeshInstance3D per pattern, painted by the surface shader. A shape's last argument is a style dict (`params`, `accent`, `snow`); surface `"glass"` is window glass. Set `kit.frame` to build a part in its own frame. Never build a house from one MeshInstance3D per box again.
- **Painted per vertex:** by default (`kit.paint_per_vertex`) every color, accent and snow amount of one pattern (and params) shares one mesh: the vertex color is the base, CUSTOM0 the accent, the vertex alpha the snow, drawn by `Surfaces.painted(pattern, params)`. A house is 5 to 9 draw calls (`Constants.ART_HOUSE_MAX_DRAW_CALLS`, 10, checked by mesh_check). Turn it off for something repainted by swapping its material (a fence panel the paint task refreshes): then each color is its own mesh and `Surfaces.material()`.
- **Houses come from `HouseBuilder.build(parent, at, req)`** (`levels/town/house_builder.gd`) in one of the archetypes in `levels/town/house_archetypes.gd`, facing +Z with the origin at the footprint's ground center. The returned `House` carries HouseSpec metas (windows, the climbable roof, gutters, solids, roof blockers, porch depth, door, spigot, eaves, footprint, triangles): hang gameplay on those, never on the archetype. Anything new that stands on the main roof or reaches its eaves (a dormer, a cross-gable, a hood, a chimney) is recorded as a roof blocker (`_blocker`, or `"blocker": true` on a `_gable`), and gutters and eaves split around it.
- **Footprint contract:** everything within x +-(width/2 + 0.6) and z from -depth/2 - 0.7 to depth/2 + 2.6 (`Constants.ART_HOUSE_*_REACH`), and every job archetype's main roof is a climbable gable along x. Budget: `Constants.ART_HOUSE_MAX_TRIANGLES` (6000).
- **A neighborhood's look is data** in `levels/town/hood_styles.gd`: which archetypes, palettes with their surfaces, fences, trees, ground, street, props, set pieces, and signs. Add to the data before adding code.
- Check new archetypes with `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/mesh_check.gd` (face orientation per shape, footprint, spec metas, gutter and eave clearance, budget for every archetype at the smallest and largest yard widths) and look at them in the studio.
- **A yard's house in a hood:** `HouseBuilder.request_for(hood, house_seed, size, spec := {}, neighbor := false)` turns the yard's own numbers (`LotGenerator.house_spec`: width, depth, stories, where it stands, the palette draws, the garage) into a HouseBuilder req in the hood's look; archetype, door and accent come from a separate rng (`hash([house_seed, "art"])`). The job yard and every parcel use it, so a town house and its job yard match. `HouseBuilder.spec_for(req)` gives the HouseSpec without building a node.

### Parcels, streets and blocks (levels/town/)

- **A parcel** (`LotBuilder.build(parent, xform, req)`): a house and its yard in a hood's style at any transform. Parcel frame: origin at the ground center, the street edge at +z = depth/2 (+20: the job yard's own frame), the house facing +z, frontage wide along x. Turn the parcel, never the house in it. `LotBuilder.plan(req)` decides everything as data first (house, drive, walk, beds, front fence, yard props, backyard, cars, bins, trees, grass clearings, decor spots, signature counts, art hash); `build` places it, `colliders(req)` gives `[[center, size]]` boxes and `skyline_boxes(req)` `[[center, size, color]]` for the drivable town. Lawn, drive and walk tops are `Constants.ART_LAWN_TOP` (-0.005), `ART_DRIVE_TOP`, `ART_WALK_TOP`, above the Surroundings ground's -0.02. A narrow lot (Dandelion Acres' 22 m) hugs its home to one side with the drive up the other; a 40 m or wider one keeps the job yard's layout (drive at x 0 from z 3). A parcel's root is `levels/town/parcel.gd` (group `seasonal`), which rebuilds its `SeasonDecor` when the season turns.
- **A street** (`StreetBuilder.segment(parent, from, to, style, opts)`): the road (painted `road` pattern, or `gravel`), curbs, sidewalks or a gravel shoulder, the grass strip, lamps (staggered, both sides) or power poles with their wires as one mesh, storm drains, speed bumps only past `ART_SPEED_BUMP_FROM_X`. Repeats go into the caller's PropBatch (`opts.batch`, `opts.batch_xform`). Metas `lamps`, `poles`, `curbs`, `length`. `StreetBuilder.intersection()` is a crosswalk patch with stop signs.
- **A block** (`BlockBuilder.build(parent, xform, hood, seed, season, opts)` / `build_for_job(lot, hood, job_seed, yard)`): the street, parcels both sides (near side from x +-(21.2 + frontage/2), far side turned PI around z 56), the hood's set pieces, side hedges or fences at every parcel edge, the back boundary at z -24, street trees, parked cars, grass and the block's season decor, built `ART_BLOCK_PARCELS_PER_FRAME` parcels a frame. Neighbor seeds are `hash([seed, "neighbor", slot])`. It tops up each prop-based signature group (`TOPUP`) so a hood's look never depends on luck, keeps Chefty's lane (`ART_CHEFTY_LANE`) clear and leaves a far lot in the NICE BOYS van's way out vacant (a FOR SALE sign). One HouseKit carries every parcel's lawn, drive and walk, and one PropBatch every repeated prop, so a block is about 400 to 530 draw calls with its grass.
- **Set pieces** (`levels/town/set_pieces.gd`): `sign_post`, `neighborhood_watch_sign`, `entrance_sign` (monument, arch or wooden), `hoa_gatehouse`, `hoa_rules_board`, `park_office`, `cluster_mailboxes`, `lake` (a `parcel.gd` "lake": water, sand, far-shore pines, frozen with a shanty in winter), `docks_for`, `terrace` (Mansion Hill's 3 m retaining wall, raised lawn and stairs), `back_boundary` (wood fence, cinderblock, stone wall, the HOA wall with its painted-over rectangles, or iron). Each joins `art_signature:<id>`.
- **Season decor** (`levels/town/season_dressing.gd`): `decorate(parcel, decor, style, season, seed, lights)` and `block_decor(block, season, decor)`, every node in `season_decor` and `art_signature:season_<id>`.
- **Check** with `tools/block_check.gd` (run by mesh_check): request_for archetypes, sign keys, every hood's job block (determinism, signatures, the visual count, Chefty's lane by MultiMesh instance, houses inside parcels), and Maple Row in four seasons. `PropBatch` views keep `instance_xforms` because the headless renderer forgets MultiMesh data.

### Props: the catalog and batches

- **Every neighborhood prop comes from `PropCatalog`** (`props/prop_catalog.gd`): `mesh(kind, color, size)`, `node(kind, color, size)` for one MeshInstance3D, `place(parent, kind, xform, color, opts)` for a prop with its extras, `kinds()`, `SIMPLE`. The models live in `props/street_props.gd` (mailboxes, bins, lamps, power poles and wires, signs, speed bumps, bollards, benches, hoops, and every car), `props/yard_extras.gd` (grills, swing sets, trampolines, flamingos, bikes, chairs, laundry, junk, sheds of the playhouse kind, fence runs, boxwoods, the barn and silo), `props/estate_props.gd` (pools, docks, boats, fountains, topiaries including the rake, lions, iron fences and gates, columns, the gatehouse, violation flags, monument signs, the arch, retaining walls, the fishing shanty) and `props/season_props.gd` (pumpkins to pool floats). Shorthand shapes are in `props/prop_parts.gd`; use them, not MeshKit's detail-3 `rbox` (1,280 triangles a call).
- **Frame:** origin at the ground center, the front toward -Z. `Color.WHITE` and `Vector3.ONE` mean the kind's defaults. Sized kinds (each builder's `SIZED`) read size as dimensions: runs are size.x long, a pool's water size.x by size.z, a dock size.z long toward -Z. Icicles, string lights and wreaths hang from their origin.
- **Many of a kind go in a `PropBatch`** (`props/prop_batch.gd`): `add(kind, xform, color, size)` then `commit()`; one MultiMesh per 32 m chunk and mesh. Only `SIMPLE` kinds batch; lamps, pools, fountains, boats with names, signs with words, jack-o'-lanterns and string lights go through `place()`, which adds their glow, water, bulbs or Label3D. Small props fade at 60 m and cast no shadow, the rest at 140 m, big ones never (`PropCatalog.fade()`).
- **Words** come from `Strings.SIGNS` by key (`opts.sign`) or `opts.text`; `PropCatalog.LABELS` says where each kind's words go. Boat names come from `Strings.BOAT_NAMES`. No art text says "ice" (docs/GAME.md).
- **Water** is `props/water_surface.gd`: `material("pool"|"lake"|"fountain"|"frozen")`, `plane()`, `disc()`, `sheet()` (a falling ribbon), `lake_kind(season)`.
- **Budget:** every prop under `Constants.ART_PROP_MAX_TRIANGLES` (2,000). Check with `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/prop_check.gd` (also run by `tools/mesh_check.gd`): budgets, winding against stored normals, `place()` extras, style references, sign words, batching. For a thin two-sided card or leaf use `PropParts.card()` / `PropParts.leaf()`, not MeshKit's `leaf_card` (its back faces carry the front normal).
### Surfaces (props/surfaces.gd)

- Say what a mesh is made of with `LowPoly.paint(node, surface, params, accent)`; `levels/shared_look.gd` paints it from the mesh metas `surface`, `surface_base`, `surface_params` (Vector4) and `surface_accent` (Color). Patterns are measured in meters in the mesh's own space: walls use the vertical plane, floors and roofs use x and z.
- Patterns: plain, grass, asphalt, concrete, brick, siding, shingles, planks, bark, fabric, metal, dirt, foliage, skin, stucco, stone (fieldstone; `params.x` 1 = cut ashlar), roof_tiles (clay barrel rows), corrugated (vertical ribs and rust streaks, walls or roofs), shakes (cedar), board_batten, gravel, pavers (`params.x` 1 = herringbone), lawn_stripes (`params.x` = contrast), cinderblock, tile_floor (polished), road.
- **Road markings are painted, not modeled.** The `road` pattern is asphalt plus lines on the top face, in local space (along = local x, across = local z). `params`: x = line style, y = half the road's width, z = dash length, w = dash period (0 = twice the dash). Styles (`Surfaces.ROAD_LINES`): 0 none, 1 dashed double yellow, 2 white edges, 3 both, 4 parking stalls every 3 m, 5 an intersection patch with crosswalk bars along each edge (y = half the patch, z = band depth), 6 single dashed yellow (country roads). `Surfaces.road_params(style, half_width, dash, period)` builds the Vector4. A whole street is one mesh.
- Lawns: `params.z` on grass or lawn_stripes is a flower floor that stays all year (Dandelion Acres).

### Seasons in the art

- `Seasons.apply_look(day)` runs on every peer and sets three shader globals: `season_foliage` (the old crown and grass tint), `season_snow` (0.85 in winter) and `season_bloom` (0.8 in spring, 0.15 in summer). Declare any new global in `project.godot` `[shader_globals]` in the same commit as the shader that reads it, or every material fails to compile.
- Every surface (props/surfaces.gd) gets snow on faces that point up, in soft drifts; asphalt, roads and gravel only get a grey slush; skin and tile_floor never do. Lawns get flower dots from the bloom.
- **Indoors opts out.** Surfaces snow is on by default and turned down per mesh: `Surfaces.material(pattern, color, accent, params, snow)` (0-1, default 1), set from the mesh meta `surface_snow` or `LowPoly.paint(node, surface, params, accent, snow)`. Pass 0 for anything under a roof (the Homie Depot lobby, garage floors, porches).
- Vertex-color meshes (`props/stylized.gd`) only get snow when their material opts in: option `snow` (0-1, default 0), so characters, the first-person arms and held things never turn white. Trees and every prop catalog kind (`PropCatalog.options()`) opt in; YardModels' own nodes (the hide-spot shed, dumpster and hedge, fences, the yard's cars) still stay clear until a later art chunk passes `snow: 1.0` for its outdoor kinds. Option `seasonal: false` stops the foliage tint for meshes that are built per season (tree crowns).
- When the season changes (and once at start), every node in group `seasonal` gets `apply_season(season)` to rebuild or swap itself. `Seasons.current()` is the season showing; `Seasons.first_day_of(season)` is a day in it.

### Trees (props/trees.gd)

- Kinds `Trees.KINDS`: broadleaf, pine, birch, willow, cypress. Crowns `Trees.CROWNS`: the autumn ones callers pick (orange, red, yellow, rust, turning, green) and the season ones (blossom_pink, blossom_white, spring_green, deep_green). Evergreens ignore the crown.
- **Built per season:** `Trees.seasonal_form(kind, crown, season)` gives `[kind, crown shown, form]`, form `full`, `bare` (broadleaf kinds in winter) or `snowy` (evergreens in winter). `Trees.mesh_for(kind, crown, variant, season = current, far = false)`; `Trees.form_mesh(kind, crown, variant, shown_crown, form, far)` for one exact form. The shape comes from (kind, autumn crown, variant) alone; only the leaves change with the season.
- **`Trees.spawn(...)`** returns a `props/tree_node.gd` root in group `seasonal` (metas `kind`, `form`, `crown`) with `Look` and `LookFar`. LookFar draws from `Constants.ART_TREE_LOD_DISTANCE` (55 m) and Look hangs off it through `visibility_parent`, so exactly one draws. Far meshes are the trunk, the main limbs and a few detail-1 blobs over the crown's clumps, never leaf cards.
- **Budgets:** near within `Constants.ART_TREE_MAX_TRIANGLES` (7,000), far within `ART_TREE_FAR_MAX_TRIANGLES` (900), recorded as mesh meta `triangles`. Leaf cards are cut to fit (`_card_count`), and tip clumps that nearly overlap merge. `tools/mesh_check.gd` checks every kind x crown (evergreens too: the crown is part of the seed) x variant (-3..3: callers pass negative ones and `variant % VARIANTS` keeps the sign) x season, near and far, the exact season mapping, and a tree node's LOD wiring, swap and prewarm. It is the `meshcheck` step in `ci_local` and `netcode.yml` (no port).
- **Season turns:** the day before a turn `Seasons.apply_look` calls `prewarm_season(next)` on `seasonal` nodes that have it, and each tree builds its next meshes into the cache within a shared per-frame budget (`Constants.ART_TREE_PREWARM_MS_PER_FRAME`), so the turn only swaps. `apply_season` clears Look's `visibility_parent` while it swaps both meshes and then restores it: changing LookFar's mesh walks its dependents, and the renderer rejects (`dep_instance->array_index == -1`) a dependent whose mesh changed, or that was spawned, that same frame.
- Look at them with the studio subjects `trees_new`, `trees_seasons` (render with `--season=1` too, to see the snow settle) and `tree_lod` (each kind's near and far mesh side by side, from 55 m).

### The studio

- `Godot --path . res://tools/studio/studio.tscn -- --subject=<name> --out=build/art/<name>.png`, with the windowed (or console, not headless) build, since the dummy renderer compiles no shaders. Grep its output for `SHADER ERROR`.
- Options: `--season=0..3` (autumn, winter, spring, summer; set before the subject is built), `--hood=<id>`, `--size=0..2`, `--seed=N`, `--archetype=<id>` for the neighborhood subjects, and any `--key=value` a subject reads.
- Subjects: trees, tree (`--kind --crown --variant`, `--far=1` for the far mesh), trees_new (birch, willow and cypress in --season, then bare, snowy, blossom and summer forms), trees_seasons (one row per kind, one column per season, each a spawned tree turned with `apply_season`; `--row=N` for one row), tree_lod (near and far pairs seen from 55 m), characters, character, props, small_props, truck, van, surfaces (every pattern as a labeled cube, ground swatches, and a road strip per line style; render it in all four seasons after touching the shader), houses (every archetype in a labeled lineup, each in a palette from a neighborhood that builds it) and house (one: `--archetype`, else the first of `--hood`'s). Both take `--width=min|max|<m>`, `--depth=<m>`, `--stories=1|2`, `--detail=far`, `--seed`, `--size`. Render houses at `--width=min` and `--width=max`, autumn and winter, after touching an archetype.
- Prop subjects: street_props, cars, yard_extras, estate, seasonal_props (each a labeled lineup, one row per view seen from the front; `--page=1` for the next four rows, `--view=back` for the backs) and water (pool, hot tub, fountain, kiddie pool, birdbath, a lake with a dock, boats, a swim platform and a buoy), plus prop (one: `--kind=<id>`, `--color=<html>`, `--lights=homemade` for string lights). Render them autumn and winter (`--season=1`: snow on top, none underneath, the lake and pools frozen) after touching a prop.
- Neighborhood subjects: parcel (one LotBuilder parcel: `--hood --size --seed --season`), hood_lineup (one parcel per hood, side by side), block (the job's street block for `--hood` in `--season`, Chefty's lane outlined in red; views down the street, the lane from overhead, a neighbor's yard, and across the street or, for Lakeside, the lake) and street (a segment in every hood's street style). After touching a builder, render `block` for all six hoods in all four seasons and read every PNG.
- The studio's ground sits at -0.02, where the game's Surroundings ground is, so parcels' lawns show over it.
- A subject picks its own camera by setting meta `views` on its root: an Array of `{from, at, fov}`, replacing the default four views in order.

## Game-specific notes

- **The store is Homie Depot.** Signage, the store zone, and any store UI use that name.
- **Trucks.** Any truck model uses the Chefty logo. Do not invent a second truck brand.
- **Leaves are the star.** They are rendered in bulk from the leaf field, so leaf art means a handful of leaf meshes or cards with color and size variation, not thousands of unique assets. A full lot should read as overwhelming from the truck, and a raked strip should read as satisfying.
- **The NICE BOYS (the enforcement crew) are fictional, and their look is decided** (OQ-012, docs/GAME.md "2026-11-01, the NICE BOYS' look"). They read as overzealous HOA, parking enforcement and mall security rolled into one, aggressively pleasant: furious grins under angry brows.
  - **Their colors:** Courtesy Lavender `Color(0.74, 0.70, 0.93)` (`NiceSeal.LAVENDER`) polos and visors, khaki shorts, white knee socks and sneakers, gold aviators, a red lanyard, a teal fanny pack. Lavender is pastel and stays apart from the players' shirt colors, Marty's orange apron, the hires and TurfMasters™ green.
  - **Their insignia is the Daisy Seal** (`props/nice_seal.gd`): a smiling six-petal daisy on a scalloped lavender rosette with two ribbon tails, a "Certified Nice" sticker. Anything that shows the NICE BOYS (patches, the visor, the van, the beacon, posters, notices) uses `NiceSeal.add()` or `NiceSeal.node()`, never a new emblem.
  - **Never:** shields, stars, badges shaped like a law-enforcement badge, navy or black uniforms, tactical gear, light bars, or acronyms. NICE is never spelled out. Our own fictional lettering (THE NICE BOYS, "Courtesy Patrol") is fine; a real agency's name, logo, insignia or lettering never is.
  - **Built in code:** `CrewMember.dress(body, index)` dresses a `player/cartoon_body.gd` body (`outfit` "nice_boy", or "nice_boy_lead" for Crew1, who carries a megaphone and a whistle instead of a clipboard). Outfit parts use blob detail 0 or 1, boxes, and tubes of 8 sides or fewer, never `rbox`. The only exceptions are tubes that cover a body part and match its sides so it can't poke through: the shorts, socks and sock stripe (10, like the plain leg), the collar and visor band (12), and the megaphone (10). docs/DECISIONS.md records the cost. See them with the studio subjects `nice_boys` and `nice_boy` (`--index=1` for the lead, `--gesture=point` or `scribble`).
- **The cartoon body is over the character budget.** A plain body measures 8,082 triangles against the 5,000 below (`--cast-test` step 9; the detail-3 head and hair and the detail-2 eyes, brows, mouth and shoes). The NICE BOYS' outfit adds about 900 to 970 more, which the test holds under `Constants.CREW_OUTFIT_TRIANGLE_BUDGET` (1,200). Bringing the base body under budget is a separate task.
- **The van has to be countable.** Players must be able to count the captured players inside it at a glance, so plan for windows, an open side door, or an exaggerated cutaway.

## Technical rules

- Scale is 1 unit = 1 meter. A player is about 1.8 meters tall.
- Y is up. Models face -Z, which is Godot's forward direction. The origin sits at the bottom center.
- Models are `.glb`. Short sound effects are `.wav`, and music and ambience are `.ogg`. Textures are `.png`, usually 256 px or smaller, never above 1024 px.
- Budgets: props under 2,000 triangles, characters under 5,000.
- Strip embedded cameras and lights from imported models.

## Sources

- CC0 packs such as Kenney and Quaternius are the first choice for filler.
- Mixamo for character rigging and animation.
- AI image-to-3D or text-to-3D generators for specific props. Generated models nearly always need decimating to the triangle budget, rescaling, and recoloring to the palette before they go in.

## Logging

Every third-party or AI-generated asset gets a row in `assets/LICENSES.md` when it is added: file, source, license, whether it was AI-generated, and which tool made it. This matters for two reasons. We need to honor licenses, and Steam's content survey asks developers to disclose AI-generated content that players see or hear when the game is submitted. AI tools used only during development, such as coding assistants, are not the focus of that disclosure. The log is what we will fill the survey out from, so keep it current.

## Icons

Every UI icon is a committed PNG in `assets/icons/<id>.png`, generated from the game's own code models and code-drawn shapes by `tools/studio/icon_studio.gd`. Never hand-edit one: change its recipe and regenerate it. `ui/icons.gd` is the only loader (`texture`, `rect`, `row`, `bbcode`, `rich`, `plain` and the `for_*` id helpers); a missing icon comes back as null or an empty spacer, never an error.

- **Sizes.** 256 px square for 3D items, tools, upgrades, task badges and rank badges; 128 px for flat icons; keycaps 64 px tall (128 wide for Tab, Esc, Space, Shift, Ctrl, Alt, Enter and Backspace); the app icon 256 px plus `app_icon.ico` (16, 32, 48 and 256). Nothing is ever larger than 256 px on a side (`Icons.MAX_PX`).
- **Flat icons.** Drawn in code by the `draw_*` funcs in `icon_recipes.gd` in units of the icon's height ((0, 0) top left, (1, 1) bottom right; a wide key runs to x = 2), kept inside about 0.06..0.92 so the outline and shadow fit. Inner ink lines are 0.02 of the size so shapes still separate at 20 px; layered shapes get an ink edge, and white pictograms on a tile get a tile-colored one. Text is the fallback font emboldened (`FontVariation`), centered on its cap height. Reuse the helpers (`_maple`, `_leaf`, `_tile`, `_pin`, `_face`, `_crew_head`, `_person`) so a new icon matches its family.
- **Camera and light.** The camera looks along (0.75, 0.55, 1.0) toward the subject (front-right, a little above), FOV 30, framed to the corners of each of the subject's meshes (not one world box, which would frame a diagonal tool far too loosely) with 8% padding (3% inside a badge's emblem area). Long, thin tools (ladder, hedge trimmer, paint roller, leaf blower) also carry a `roll` that turns the camera about its view axis so they lie corner to corner: a tool icon is used at 16 to 20 px, and a long axis laid flat across the square shrinks to a smear there. Models face -Z, so a recipe's `yaw` of about 180 shows the front. One warm key light (`SharedLook.SUN_COLOR`, rotation (-40, -30, 0), energy 1.25), a cool fill from the other side (Color(0.75, 0.85, 1.0), 0.35), flat warm ambient light (0.55), filmic tonemap at exposure 1.05. No sky, fog, SSAO, glow or shadows. The season globals are set to autumn first.
- **Sticker finish.** 3D renders at 2x and flat drawing at 4x, then a GPU pass composites the layers (badge disc or shield, the 3D render, the upgrade sticker) and dilates the alpha into an ink outline (Color(0.17, 0.14, 0.11), size / 85: 3 px at 256) with a drop shadow (down size / 64, 25% alpha), and the result is shrunk with Lanczos. A badge's 3D vignette also gets its own ink ring (0.8 of the outline) so it sits on the disc like a sticker; The Lawnfather's ring is gold.
- **Generate.** `Godot --path . res://tools/studio/icon_studio.tscn -- [--only=id1,id2] [--out=assets/icons] [--sheet=build/art/icons_sheet.png]`, then `Godot --headless --path . --import` once. It needs a window (it can't run headless or in CI) and forces each draw, so a covered window is fine. Use `--only` when changing one icon, so GPU or driver differences never churn the others. A new PNG gets a minimal `.import` sidecar (mipmaps on, detect 3D off, no uid) and `--import` fills in the uid; commit both.
- **Judge it.** `--sheet` writes every icon at 96 px and 20 px with its id, on paper and on a dark panel. Read the PNG and check outline consistency, legibility at 20 px on both halves, clipped silhouettes and dark fringes.
- **Add an icon.** Put the id in `ui/icons.gd` `IDS` (following the naming rules there) and a recipe with the same key in `tools/studio/icon_recipes.gd` `RECIPES` (a builder for 3D, a draw func for flat), run the generator with `--only=<id>`, import, look at the sheet, commit the PNG and its `.import`. Then run `tools\ci_local.ps1 -Only icons -SkipSoak`: the icons test fails when an id has no PNG or no recipe, or when a tool, task, app, hood, mood, season, rank, unlock or rebindable key has no icon (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, "UI icons are committed PNGs").

## Audio

Goofy, not realistic. The reference is Schedule I: bouncy, cartoony, a little dumb. Taylan set this on 2026-10-19 after hearing realistic synthesized sounds and not liking any of them.

- Sound effects are cartoon sounds: pomfs, swooshes, boings, honks, slide whistles, brass stabs. Exaggerate them so they read as jokes, not as foley.
- Characters never speak real words. They make short silly noises (ah?, oof, eh?, huh?, hup) and babble gibberish when talking, like the "ewewewew" of Animal Crossing. Each character's pitch differs a little so they can be told apart.
- Every sound is synthesized by `tools/make_sfx.py`, so all audio is a project original with nothing to license or disclose. Change a sound by changing its recipe there and re-running it.
- Proximity voice chat between real players is untouched: that is real people talking.
- Every sound id is registered in `audio/sound_bank.gd` (its variants, bus, gap, length limit, and volume trim). `--audio-test` checks that each id's files exist and load, and scans the code for sound ids and `res://assets/audio/` paths that aren't registered or don't exist. A new sound is a recipe in `tools/make_sfx.py` or `tools/make_loops.py` plus a bank entry.
- Gameplay sounds are positional (`Sfx.play_at`, 60 m reach); only interface sounds and the HUD's cues are 2D (`Sfx.play`).
- Musical guardrail: no Latin-American or regional genre signifiers in any cue (no mariachi trumpets, bossa, salsa, cumbia, ranchera, or guiro- or maraca-led grooves). Jokes in sound punch up at work, systems, and bureaucracy.
- Music and stingers are `.ogg` files in `assets/audio/music/`, synthesized by `tools/make_music.py` (each song is data at the bottom of it: sections, chords, patterns, melodies) and encoded by `tools/encode_ogg.py` (Godot's Movie Maker on this machine; docs/DECISIONS.md, "Audio: the music pipeline"). Loudness targets: loops sit at -19 dBFS RMS and nothing peaks over -1 dBFS; stingers are at most -18 dBFS RMS. A loop is a whole number of 20 ms frames and goes back to the end of its intro, not to the top.
- Reviewing sound without ears: `python tools/audio_check.py <id> --png` prints the numbers and violations and draws `build/audio/png/<id>.png` (waveform, sections, loop point, 8 frequency bands), and `python tools/encode_ogg.py --verify <id>` checks the encoded loop the way Godot plays it. Iterate with `make_music.py <id> --preview` or `--section B` (22.05 kHz mono). Taylan listens through the jukebox (audio chunk 7) or by opening the .ogg.

### Rake and leaf sounds (gap R1, raking:5)

Taylan called the old rake sound terrible after playing 0.4.5 (a synth swoosh rising 500 to 4000 Hz, played 2D for the raker only, three nearly identical files overlapping into a 3.3 Hz hiss). The rake and the leaves are now **physically motivated** instead: each sound models what makes the noise in a yard, exaggerated only in level and clarity. This is the one place the "goofy, not realistic" rule bends (the integrator's call for R1); everything else stays cartoon. Recipes are `tools/sfx_leaves.py` on primitives in `tools/sfx_grains.py` (stdlib only); `python tools/make_sfx.py <ids>` writes them (the `SOUNDS_RMS` table: variants, RMS target, length), at an RMS target rather than a peak, soft-clipped under -1 dBFS.

- **The models.** A struck thing is damped modes (`modal_click`). Dry leaves crumpling are a Poisson stream of click clusters with power-law (Pareto) amplitudes, each click a leaf's short resonance with a partial 1.6-2.3x up, and some events bigger, lower folds (`crackle`; after the crumpling-noise studies of Houle and Sethna, Kramer and Lobkovsky). Tines dragged over ground stick and slip: each of a dozen tines releases quasi-periodically, more often and higher the faster it moves (`stick_slip`), so a scrape falls in pitch as the stroke slows. Ground under the head is brown rumble plus low knocks over clods. One speed curve per stroke (plant, push, lift, a hand's tremor, snags where tines catch) drives every layer, so they move together; every variant is its own stroke (its own curve, snags and grains), and plant variants land differently (flat, heel-toe, skid then dig in, late toe, a bounce in deep litter, a twig snapping).
- **The layers of one swing.** `rake_plant` (6 variants, 0.14 s, -24 dBFS RMS: soil knock, frame clack, tines landing, a first crunch) where the head lands; `rake_scrape` (8, 0.30 s, -27) 15 ms later, pitched to the swing's tempo, or `rake_empty` (4, 0.18 s, -32: a quiet swish, nothing crackles) when the stroke moved nothing; a leaf layer for every bite that moves at least `LEAF_SFX_MIN_UNITS`, `rake_leaves_light/_med/_heavy` (6 each; 0.30/0.42/0.55 s; -26/-23/-20) by the leaves moved against `LEAF_SFX_REF_UNITS` (edges `LEAF_SFX_TIERS`) at -12 dB up to 0 (capped +2); `leaf_flick` (4, 0.22 s, -27) at the end of the drive after the mid-drive bite.
- **Everything else.** `hand_scoop` (4, -28) for hand raking; `bag_open` (3, -24) then `bag_stuff` (4, 0.60 s, -21, louder for a bigger pile) at +`BAG_STUFF_DELAY` then the old `bag` tie at +`BAG_TIE_DELAY` (all three from LeafAudio on every peer, the host too: `world._spawn_bag` leaves the tie to it once the field tells strokes, or the host would hear the tie first), heard by everyone; `leaf_settle` (6, -31) and `pile_whump` (3, -24) from the leaf motion layer's landings; `leaf_kick` (4, -27) for kicks, dives, scatters and the plow; `step_leaves_deep` and `step_leaves_pile` (4 each, -29) for audio:6's footsteps (`LeafAudio.step_id`). The old `rake` id stays (3, 0.45 s, -25: plant, scrape and light leaves in one file) for anything that plays a stroke as one sound; nothing in the rake plays it any more. The blower and the mower play nothing here: their loops cover them.
- **Triggers.** `audio/leaf_audio.gd` (a child of Sfx, group `leaf_audio`) plays all of it from the leaf field's `stroke_event` (gap-specs/raking.json contract C6): the event is the bite on every peer, so the raker, the host and every observer hear the same stroke at the same moment as its leaves move, from where it happened (`play_at`, `RAKE_SFX_UNIT_SIZE` 3.5 m, `RAKE_SFX_MAX_DISTANCE` 30 m), keyed per raker so two rakers never silence each other. Leaf ids get a ±3.5% pitch wobble, ±1.5 dB, and a shuffle bag (every variant once per refill). Tuning: the `# ---- raking: sounds` constants.
- **Mix targets for audio:8.** The RMS figures above are the targets (per file, before the bank's `db`, which is 0 for all of them). The relative levels (plant over scrape, the heavy layer loudest, landings and the bare swish quietest) are the design; audio:8 sets where the whole group sits against footsteps and tools.
- **Checking without ears.** `python tools/rake_audio_check.py` checks every id against those targets (RMS ±1.5 dB, length, no clipping, no two variants sharing a loudness envelope, leaf layers spiky enough to be crackle and not hiss, the plant's peak in its first 5 ms, weight under 300 Hz in the plant and scrape, the scrape's pitch falling); `--png` draws spectrograms into `build/r1/audio/`, and `--preview` writes how the game layers them (`preview_swing`, `preview_held` three swings, `preview_bare`, `preview_pile`, `preview_bag`). Agents can only measure: **Taylan's ears are the acceptance gate.** The listening list is those five previews, then `rake_plant_1..6`, `rake_scrape_1..8`, `rake_leaves_med_1..6` and `bag_stuff_1..4`. The old files are in git history for a quick revert.
