# Playtesting a moment

How an agent judges the game the way a player does: in motion, with sound, from more than one seat. One screenshot can't show a leaf teleporting, a camera freezing, a catch with no gotcha or a sound landing late. A player-facing change isn't done until it has been recorded and walked through the checklist below (docs/GAME.md, 2026-11-02).

## The loop

```powershell
tools\play.ps1 -Start -Observer -Scenario rake_pile    # a host at the moment, plus a second window watching
tools\play.ps1 -Instance observer 'follow host 4 1.6 80; record_start 30 rake_3p'
tools\play.ps1 'record_start 30 rake_1p; wait 0.3; hold primary 1.3; wait 0.6; record_stop'
tools\play.ps1 -Instance observer 'record_stop'
tools\frames.ps1 build\play\rec_rake_1p "10..16"       # read a stretch of frames up close
tools\play.ps1 -Stop
```

Then read, with the Read tool: `rec_<label>\sheet.png` (the overview), `sheet_01.png ...` (every frame), `audio.png` (the timeline), `events.json` (what happened on each frame), `sync.json` (each sound's lag), and single `frame_NNN.png` files or `tools\frames.ps1` grids for detail. Recordings stay in `build\play\` and `build\play-<instance>\` (git-ignored) until removed by hand; a frame is about 1 MB.

- **Start:** `tools\play.ps1 -Start` opens a windowed host with remote play (`net/dev/remote_play.gd`) on a fixed step: the engine's `--fixed-fps 30` and the game's `--fixed-step=30`, so every frame is exactly 1/30 s of game time and the game is held to real time (`net/dev/pacer.gd`); `-Fps` changes it, `-Fps 0` turns it off. `-Scenario <name>` jumps to a moment as soon as the world is up and adds the flags it needs (a job yard, or `--town-map` for `drive`). `-Observer` adds a second window that joins as a client and follows the host. It goes where the host goes (the host's remote play sends it into the host's zone whenever the host changes zones, so its HUD, cues, music and the yard's quips are a player's there, and a scenario that moves the host brings it along); its own body is a ghost (drawn by nobody, solid to nobody), the NICE BOYS never take it, and it is a watcher (`Net.set_watcher`): left out of every zone roster, so it never rides Chefty home, keeps a raid rolling or reacts to a long toss. While the host rides the van or crosses the border it waits at the store. `-Extra` passes more game flags (`--sim-lag=150 --sim-loss=2` for a bad network; the observer gets the `--sim-` ones too). `-Mute` silences the speakers (recordings still capture the mix). Each worktree gets its own port and folders, and `-Stop` closes only the windows it started.
- **A friend:** `-Friend` adds a third window, a client named Friend that plays (not parked, not a ghost, not kept safe). The moments with a second player in them need it, not the observer, who only watches: `cite_rescue`, `van_unbuckle`, `annex_visit` (`tools\play.ps1 -Start -Friend -Observer -Scenario ...`: with `-Friend` the scenario runs as a command once everyone has joined, not at the host's launch; adding `-Friend` to a running session joins it). Every other scenario parks the friend at the store; those three put it where it belongs. Record its own view with `-Instance friend`.
- **Commands:** `tools\play.ps1 '<steps>'` sends steps to the host (`-Instance observer` or `-Instance friend` for the other windows) and prints what's on screen. Steps are JSON (`'[["hold","primary",1.3]]'`) or plain (`'hold primary 1.3; look 200 0'`). Input: `hold`, `tap`, `down`, `up`, `look`, `click`, `wait`, `text` (net/dev/remote_play.gd's header lists them). A `look` over 100 px is eased over several frames like a hand on a mouse, so a `cam_snap` in your recording is the game's, not your input.
- **Scripts (pace without your thinking time):** every `tools\play.ps1` call costs real seconds while the game runs on, so a take driven call by call can't measure pace (an Annex visit by hand used most of its 150 s clock). `tools\play.ps1 -Script build\x.txt` sends a whole sequence at once: one step per line, `#` comments, and lines starting `observer:` or `friend:` go to that window; all windows get their lists together and run side by side. In-game steps that react without coming back: `until <field> <text> [timeout]` (a field of the result, dotted for nested ones: `until zone BORDER 20`, `until raid CHASE 30`, `until annex.stage inside`, `until prompt !Stamp`), `mark <text>` (a `mark` event on the frame, and `marks` with game times in the result: stopwatch your pace with them), `face x z` and `goto x z [timeout]` or `goto <path under World>` (turns with the mouse and walks with move_forward in a straight line: no pathfinding, walls stop it). Example:
  ```
  observer: follow host 5 1.8 60
  observer: record_start 15 cite_3p
  observer: until raid CHASE 30
  observer: wait 14
  observer: record_stop
  record_start 30 cite_1p
  scenario cite_rescue
  mark thrower ready
  hold drop 0.45
  wait 3.5
  record_stop
  ```
- **Scenarios** (host only, dev builds only; `net/dev/scenarios.gd`): `rake_fresh` (untouched leaves ahead, rake in hand), `rake_pile` (a raked pile 2.4 m ahead), `raid_warn` (the NICE BOYS called now: 8 s of warning), `raid_arrive` (they arrive in 2 s, facing the arrival point), `caught` (the chase on, the head NICE BOY 5 m away), `hide` (a hiding spot in front of you, 3.5 s before they arrive), `van_seated` (buckled into the parked Courtesy Shuttle, door open: the seat view, the knock, the mirror checks), `van_unbuckle` (a friend buckled in and you at the door; needs -Friend), `van_ride` (buckled in, it pulls out in 2 s: door, blinker, drive, iris), `van_straggler` (it leaves with a NICE BOY still out: the creep and the bumper hop; you watch from behind), `dropoff` (aboard the return shuttle pulling up at the yard's curb), `annex` (through Annex B's door behind the Greeter, Form 27-B in hand, a step back from your first box's window; `border_return` is its old name for one wave), `annex_break` (that window's clerk telegraphs his break in 2 s), `annex_heist` (he's looking away and you're at his stamp), `annex_bigstamp` (the Head NICE BOY just left, a full form, his big stamp in front of you), `annex_window_d` (Window D's blinds go up in 2 s), `annex_visit` (the second player, the friend, is at the Annex; you're at the visitor door: the Field Office's in the town, the Courtesy Desk by Homie Depot otherwise), `annex_closing` (closing time in 10 s), `store`, `drive` (Chefty's driver's seat; needs the connected town), `toss` (a leaf bag in your hands 4 m from Chefty's bed, facing it: `hold drop 0.7` tosses it in), `toss_full` (the same with Chefty already full, so it bounces off the heap), `toss_close` (a bag in hand 2 m from the bed, by the side wall: the hop over the rail, and E). `scenario list` lists them. Each runs through the game's own code (the run director, the van, the Annex, the leaf field, the seats) after undoing whatever the last one left behind, for every player: nobody stays seated in a van or Chefty, hidden, caught or held; every window's raid lines, quip caption, stage shot and iris are cleared at once; and the director rolls no raid of its own for 2 minutes, so the roaming crew can't take your friend before the next scenario. Only where people stand is arranged, and the recorder logs that as a `placed` event, not a pop or a snap. `van_seated` holds until something says go (`van_ride` and `van_straggler` say it). `lunge`: he's already out and facing you, the wind-up comes 0.6 s after. `cite_rescue` (needs `-Friend`): the friend is cited in front of the NICE BOY, you stand 3.4 m behind him with the gnome, the friend on his far side, so `hold drop 0.45` throws it into him, never into them. `annex_heist` / `annex_bigstamp`: on the floor at the counter's lip / the desk's visitor spot, looking at the stamp.
- **Recording:** `record_start <fps> <label>` starts (it keeps going across commands, so you can record while you hold keys or wait), `record_stop` stops and writes, `record <seconds> <fps> <label>` does both around a wait. The fps is rounded to a divisor of the fixed step (30, 15, 10 ...). The label is kept as typed (lower case; `pile2_1p` stays `rec_pile2_1p`). The result lists the files, the spacing (`even`: true), `realtime_ratio` (game seconds per real second: under 1 means the machine was too busy and the run went slow-motion, still evenly stepped), `write_seconds` and `audio_padded_seconds`. Stopping never stalls the game: frames are written as they're captured and the rest (sheets, picture change, events.json) on a thread of its own while the game and the network run on; the answer waits for it (a 224 s take: 2 s). A new take may start in the same command as the last one's stop. A remote-play session also gives a silent peer 20 s before dropping it, so a window's hitch doesn't end the session.
- **Views:** `follow <who> <distance> <height> <yaw> [world]` puts a spectator orbit camera on `me`, `host`, `other`, a peer id or a name, or on `crew`, `van`, `chefty` (yaw 0 is behind them, 180 in front; it turns with them unless `world`, easing round, never whipping). It never sits inside anything: a wall, a house, a parked car or a crown between pulls it in at once (it eases back out), it cranes up over low things, thin things (a post, a trunk) may cross the view but never hold the camera, and it keeps out of people's heads. A NICE BOY or player within 2.4 m of the subject is a partner: the shot aims between them, turns side-on when the yaw would line them up (so `follow host 4 1.6 80` shows the escorted pair side by side), and picks the side with room. A subject seated in a van or Chefty is filmed from outside it, as anyone outside sees it, with a cut on the way in and out. A teleported subject is a cut too. Cuts are logged as `cam_cut` with a reason; a pull-in past something wide can still be a `cam_snap` (look at it: if it's the camera dodging, pick another yaw). `PLAYTEST_SPECTATOR_DEBUG=1` in the environment of `-Start` prints what holds it in. `cam x y z lx ly lz` is a fixed shot, `follow_off` (or `cam_off`) goes back to the player's own camera, `hud false` hides the HUD for a clean shot (quips and run cues are separate layers and stay).

## What a recording holds

- **sheet.png:** up to 48 frames picked evenly, 8 across, each stamped `#NNN 1.233S` (frame number, game seconds). A colored corner: red for a flag, yellow for a sound, cyan for another event; what fired is written along the tile's bottom (`>primary` is a key going down). `sheet_01.png` and on hold every frame when there are more than 48.
- **audio.png:** one time axis (game seconds) for everything: thumbnails at their moment, the waveform (peak blue, RMS pale), 8 octave bands (63 Hz to 8 kHz, dark is quiet), the picture change per frame (gray the whole frame, orange its lower third: where the work in front of you is), every sound the game asked for (yellow, with its id; an orange tick at the top where the mix actually got louder), the other events (cyan), the flags (red), the keys, frame numbers matching the sheet, and seconds. `tools\play.ps1` draws it with `tools/playtest_audio.py` when the recording finishes.
- **events.json:** per frame: game time `t`, its wall-clock `wall`, the player (`state`: zone, position, facing, pitch, what they hold, the hotbar, the prompt, the goal, open windows, raid and Annex status), the camera (`name`, `pos`, `fwd`, `fov`), every visible line of HUD text (`hud`), picture `change`, and the frame's `events`. Then every event with its time: `sfx` and `sfx_at` (id, where), `input`, `look`, `stroke_visual`, `chunk_changed`, `raid_phase`, `crew`, `capture`, `zone`, `ride`, `hide_spot`, `hidden`, `held`, `anim`, `border`, `cam_cut`; and the flags.
- **Flags** (hints to look at, not verdicts): `pop` (a player, NICE BOY or vehicle moved more than 0.6 m in one frame, faster than it can), `cam_snap` (the camera jumped over 1.2 m or turned over 50 degrees in a frame), `cam_frozen` (the camera stayed exactly still for 0.5 s while you looked or walked), `view_frozen` (the camera turned or moved a lot but the picture didn't change: a stale view, or a camera inside something), `visual_jump` (one frame changed four times more than the frames around it). A flag that lasts (a van crossing the map) is one entry with `frames` and `until`.
- **Event times:** every event has the game time of the frame it happened in (a physics tick, the network poll or a `_process` alike), and is filed under the first captured frame at or after it: at the session's full rate exactly that frame, at a lower rate (every k-th frame) the next kept one. Sounds are reported by `audio/sfx.gd` the moment they're asked for, not polled later. `record_stop` and whatever fires in its frame land on the last frame.
- **sync.json:** for each sound asked for, its onset in the mix and `lag_ms`: from the start of the frame that asked to the first 5 ms hop that rises 6 dB over the median of the 60 ms before. Good to about ±10 ms (5 ms hops, the audio thread's 10 ms mix blocks, and the wav's start estimated from `AudioServer.get_time_to_next_mix()`), so a few ms below zero is rounding, not a sound before its cause. Measured on 0.4.5: `bag` 7-20 ms. An onset more than about 50 ms after the motion it belongs to is late. A sound with no onset is buried, far away, too quiet, or a long bed with no attack.
- **audio.wav:** the real mix from the Master bus, 32-bit float, streamed to disk while recording and kept on the wall clock: it lasts as long as the frames (a take of 223.79 s has 223.84 s of sound), and a stretch the audio thread never mixed (a starved machine) is silence where it went missing, so every later sound still lines up (`audio_padded_seconds` says how much; mix that only came late is not padded). A headless run has no audio and draws stand-in frames.
- **Flags that aren't the game:** `placed` (a scenario moved people), `cam_cut` with a `reason` (the spectator followed a teleport, a vehicle, or your `follow`/`cam` step), `mark` (yours). The observer's own ghost body is never watched for pops. A friend's or crew's zone move at a scenario's start can still show as one pop on the observer.
- **Helper scripts:** Python can't open a script under a very long path (MAX_PATH): keep helper scripts in a short folder such as `%TEMP%\x\`. In PowerShell, give `[IO.File]` absolute paths: its current folder is not the shell's.

## The detail checklist

Walk all of it for any player-facing moment, with frame numbers. Write down what you see, not what the code says should happen.

1. **Anticipation, telegraph.** Before anything happens, is there a cue a player can read (a wind-up, a sound, a look, a countdown)? How many frames ahead?
2. **Contact, impact.** The moment itself: is there a frame where it visibly lands (a hit pose, a squash, a flash, a shake, a sound with a sharp attack)? Find that frame.
3. **Follow-through.** What happens in the frames after: does motion settle, do things move on, does the character react?
4. **Nothing pops or teleports.** Bodies, props, leaves, labels and the HUD move or fade into place. Check every `pop` and `visual_jump`, and step through the frames around every state change: does anything appear, vanish or jump in one frame? Leaves moving by rake should be seen moving.
5. **The camera never freezes or snaps unless on purpose.** Check `cam_snap`, `cam_cut`, `cam_frozen`, `view_frozen`. A cut should be a choice with a reason you can name (and usually a transition); a first-person camera should never end up inside geometry.
6. **Every state change has a visible cause.** For each zone change, capture, phase change, item lost or gained: what in the frames before it tells a player why?
7. **Sound lines up with motion within about 50 ms, and varies.** Compare yellow markers and onsets with the frame where the motion lands; repeated sounds shouldn't be identical every time (look at the bands in audio.png). Silence where something happens is a finding.
8. **What the other players see.** Record the same moment from the observer. Do they see the motion, hear the sound, understand what happened to their friend?
9. **What happens with lag.** Run again with `-Extra '--sim-lag=150 --sim-loss=2'`: what's late, what jumps, what's doubled?
10. **How it reads for a first-time player.** With only the screen and the sound, would someone new know what happened, why, and what to do next? Read the HUD text in events.json frame by frame: is it right at that moment, readable, not overlapping?
11. **The humor guardrail.** Punch up at impossible work, precarious systems and enforcement bureaucracy; never at immigrants, ethnicity, nationality or accents; never a real agency's name (docs/GAME.md, `net/dev/guardrail.gd`).

### R2:1 the stage (StageCam, staged bodies, iris, caption stack)

What to check whenever a moment uses the stage (gap-specs/nice_boys.json chunk 1; `--stage-test`, port 7826, checks the numbers):
- **One camera on every frame.** events.json never shows a frame with no camera; a switch to `CameraRig/StageCam` starts exactly where the old view was (same `camera.pos` on the switch frame) and blends; a `cam_cut` at a non-matching position is only allowed inside closed-iris frames. Blending out of first person never shows the inside of your own head (the stage camera skips your own head and body within 0.6 m of it).
- **Staged bodies.** On the owner and on the observer the staged body stays on its anchor (no float, no lag apart), starts with an ease (no `pop` flag at the stage start: the blend eases in and out) and is let go without a jump; on other peers the release eases back onto the network pose over `STAGE_RELEASE_BLEND`.

- **Iris.** The Daisy Seal rosette closes to a little daisy, holds at least 0.4 s, and opens on the new view with one `iris_swish` each way; the door shape is a panelled door with a brass knob sweeping shut.
- **Raid warning** (`raid_warn`): you face the curb, the flare is where the van parks, no countdown digits, no "they're here" line, the goal line dims away during WARNING, CHASE and DEPARTING.
